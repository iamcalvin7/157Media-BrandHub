## WHO VIRTU FERRIES IS

Virtu Ferries is the high-speed catamaran ferry connecting Malta
(Valletta Grand Harbour) and Sicily (Pozzallo) since 1988. The
crossing takes 1 hour 45 minutes. We run almost daily, year-round —
one of the very few high-speed services in the Mediterranean that
does not stop for winter. We carry passengers on foot and vehicles.

We are not a discovery. We are not a hidden gem. We are a
36-year-old institution that 250,000+ passengers use every year.
Content should feel familiar and trusted, not like it is pitching
something new.

Tagline: "Two islands. One ferry. Endless reasons to go."

Brand story: "Virtu Ferries has been connecting Malta and Sicily for
decades. Not just two ports — two cultures, two ways of life, two
islands that have more in common than most people realise. The
crossing takes 1 hour 45 minutes. What you discover on the other
side stays with you longer."

## BRAND COLOURS

- Vivid Red:             #e01814 — Accent. Urgency, alerts, logo V mark.
- Off White:             #f5f5f5 — Light surfaces and page backgrounds.
- Deep Navy:             #0d1b2a — Dark text and reversed contexts.

Logo mark anatomy:
- Red V:          Speed, strength, direction.
- Amber curve:    Warmth, hospitality, connection.
- Blue waves:     The Mediterranean, movement.

Colour hierarchy for image composition:
- Lead with Blue (#1e82b4) — sea, sky, primary UI.
- Use Amber (#f6a610) as warmth and texture.
- Red (#e01814) for energy and focal points only — never dominant.
- Dark compositions: Deep Navy (#0d1b2a) background with Blue and
  Amber as light sources.

## TYPOGRAPHY

Single typeface across all surfaces: Montserrat.
Hierarchy is created through weight — never by switching fonts.

- Light 300:      Body copy, long-form descriptions, captions.
- Regular 400:    Standard UI text, informational lists.
- SemiBold 600:   Subheadings, labels, small UI emphasis.
- ExtraBold 800:  Primary headlines (H1, H2), brand statements.

Typography rules:
- Never stretch, skew, or condense Montserrat.
- UI labels use uppercase + wide tracking. Body copy never uses
  all-caps.
- Minimum 13px for body text on digital surfaces.
- When Montserrat is unavailable, substitute with Inter or
  Source Sans Pro. Never use decorative or serif fonts.

## TONE OF VOICE

Style: Travel-forward. Editorially sharp. Warm but not gushing.
Confident but not corporate. Economy of language — no filler,
no clichés. Feels like a well-travelled friend, not a brand account.

DO:
- Use specific place names, food, seasons, and cultural moments.
- Write short, confident sentences with real rhythm.
- Earn the reader's time — every word should do work.
- Use platform-native language that does not feel scheduled.
- Mention the ferry as the obvious, easy choice — not as a pitch.
- Ground copy in real details: a dish, a town, a time of year,
  a particular crossing.
- Refer to the route as Valletta–Pozzallo or Malta–Sicily.
- Let the destination do the selling — the ferry is the means.

DON'T:
- Use "paradise", "breathtaking", "unforgettable", or "hidden gem".
- Use generic travel language that could belong to any brand.
- Write pushy CTAs that treat the reader as a conversion target.
- Use exclamation marks for enthusiasm rather than meaning.
- Lead with the company — lead with the place or the moment.
- Use hollow closers: "make memories", "embark on a journey",
  "your adventure awaits", "explore the beauty of".
- Overcomplicate — if it cannot be said simply, it is not ready.
- Say "Malta and Sicily" when "the two islands" reads better.

Voice examples:
  GOOD: "Pozzallo in the morning. Back in Valletta by afternoon.
        Bring the car or just a bag."

  GOOD: "Sicily is 1 hour 45 minutes away. Most people wait years
        to go. Most people who go come back."

  BAD:  "Embark on an unforgettable journey across the beautiful
        Mediterranean and discover the breathtaking island of Sicily!"

## MARKETS

English Market — primarily Maltese passengers crossing to Sicily.
  Audience:  Maltese locals, Maltese diaspora, English-speaking
             visitors to Malta.
  Framing:   Sicily as the obvious, easy, nearby escape. Weekend
             trips, day excursions, cultural access. Virtu as the
             trusted institution — the way it has always been done.
  Platforms: Facebook (primary), Instagram.
  Note:      Maltese audiences respond to specific Maltese references
             (Grand Harbour, specific towns, Maltese holidays and
             feast days). Do not over-explain the crossing — they
             know it.

Italian Market — primarily Sicilian and mainland Italian passengers.
  Audience:  Sicilian locals (especially Pozzallo and Ragusa
             province), mainland Italians, Italian-speaking
             visitors.
  Framing:   Malta as a short, accessible break with a different
             culture, currency, and feel. Position Virtu as the
             natural link — comfortable, fast, direct.
  Platforms: Facebook (primary), Instagram.
  Note:      Sicilian audiences are familiar with the service. Speak
             to them in their dialect of familiarity. Mainland
             Italian content should frame Malta as closer and more
             interesting than most people realise.

## CONTENT PILLARS

01 — The Virtu Experience
     Reasons to choose Virtu Ferries over any alternative. Speed,
     comfort, car flexibility, pet travel, Starlink WiFi, luggage
     freedom, daily crossings, no airport friction. Also: onboard
     moments, crew stories, passenger testimonials, sea views,
     boarding sequences, the feeling of travelling by sea.

02 — Why Sicily
     Destination-led content that sells Sicily so that Virtu is
     the natural next step. Specific towns, food, culture, seasons.
     The province of Ragusa. Pozzallo mornings. Baroque Ragusa.
     Street food in Catania. Noto in bloom.

03 — VF Recommends
     Curated local travel intel. Positions Virtu as the trusted
     guide, not just the ticket seller. What to eat, where to go,
     what to see — written with the authority of 36 years of
     crossings.

04 — The Crossing
     Immersive, sensory content about the sea itself. No hard sell.
     Let the Mediterranean do the work. Sunrise departures, evening
     returns, sea states, light on water, the feeling of being
     between two places.

05 — Operational / Flexible
     Calendar tag only — not a creative pillar. Schedules, service
     updates, disruption notices. Factual and clear. No brand voice
     layered over operational necessity.

## IMAGERY DIRECTION

Composition:
- Natural light strongly preferred. Golden hour and blue hour
  for hero shots. Avoid studio-lit or overly processed imagery.
- The sea should appear in most shots — it is the product.
- People on deck, looking out, not posed and grinning at the lens.
- Specific > generic: a recognisable Valletta skyline, the
  Pozzallo harbour wall, a Baroque street in Noto, arancini on
  a counter — not a generic "Mediterranean coast" stock shot.
- Vehicles on the car deck: honest and functional, not aspirational.
- Aerial (drone) and water-level perspectives both work well.
  Never shoot the vessel from a flat, quayside angle only.

Do use:
- Real passengers, real crew, real sea states.
- The colours of the two islands: limestone white and Malta honey,
  Sicilian terracotta and olive, the specific blue of the Channel.
- The logo colours (#1e82b4, #f6a610, #e01814) as natural
  environmental references — sea, sunlight, sun-bleached paintwork.

Avoid:
- Stock photography with people who are visibly not Mediterranean.
- Over-saturated sunset filters.
- Abstract graphic backdrops that could belong to any travel brand.
- Text-heavy compositions — let the image carry the weight.

For AI image generation, describe the scene in this order:
  1. Location (Valletta Grand Harbour / Pozzallo harbour / open sea /
     Sicily interior / Malta countryside)
  2. Vessel or setting detail (catamaran bow, upper deck, car deck,
     Grand Harbour skyline, Baroque architecture)
  3. Light quality (golden hour, morning haze, midday Mediterranean,
     dusk)
  4. Colour grounding (#1e82b4 sea, limestone whites, amber warmth)
  5. Mood (calm, purposeful, quietly joyful — never dramatic or tense)

Example AI image prompt (Midjourney / DALL·E):
  "Bow deck of a large modern catamaran on open Mediterranean water,
  wide shot, two passengers at the railing looking towards a distant
  Sicilian coastline, golden morning light, deep Mediterranean blue
  sea, no visible branding, photorealistic, cinematic"

## FLEET

Saint John Paul II — Flagship
  110m catamaran, 900 passengers. In service March 2019.
  Named after Pope John Paul II, who visited Malta in 1990.
  The largest and newest vessel in the fleet.

Jean de La Valette — Fleet vessel
  106.5m catamaran, 800 passengers. In service 2010.
  Named after Grand Master Jean Parisot de la Valette —
  the man who commanded the Great Siege of Malta in 1565
  and after whom the city of Valletta is named.

Both vessels fly the Maltese flag. Always refer to them by their
full names on first mention.

## LOGO RULES

Approved backgrounds:
- Full-colour logo: light neutral (#f5f5f5) or dark (#0d1b2a).
- White reversed logo: dark photographic or coloured backgrounds.

DO:
- Maintain generous clear space on all sides — at minimum the
  height of the 'F' in FERRIES.
- Use only the supplied files. Do not recreate the logo.

DO NOT:
- Stretch, skew, or distort the logo in any dimension.
- Place on busy photographic backgrounds without sufficient contrast.
- Recolour any element of the logo.
- Add outlines, drop shadows, glows, or effects not in the files.
- Place the logo over the sea or the sky in a way that makes the
  blue waves in the logo indistinct from the background.
